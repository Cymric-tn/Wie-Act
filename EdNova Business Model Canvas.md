# EdNova Business Model Canvas

EdNova sells annual licenses to educational institutions, serves educators and independent learners through lighter plans, and offers a capped free tier. Students learn from their professor's material in the language they think in, while educators see where the class is stuck. EdNova connects to existing LMS platforms and adds to them instead of replacing them.

## Customer segments

EdNova serves all five segments on one platform, and the first sales effort goes to private universities and training centers. They can pay, can decide faster than public systems, and match the first-year course content available today.

| Segment | Who decides or pays | Main need | Plan | Launch role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Private universities and higher education | Directors, deans, department heads | Student success in foundation courses, concept-level visibility, differentiation | Campus or Enterprise | Primary, from launch |
| Training centers and professional academies | Owners, program directors, learning managers | Skill pathways, cohort tracking, practice, AI support | Campus or Educator | Primary, from launch |
| High schools and school networks | School directors, private school groups | Structured revision, teacher oversight, learning analytics | Campus | Expansion, after minor-safeguards are in place |
| Independent educators and tutors | The educator | Simple class setup, shared resources, exercise banks, learner tracking | Educator | Self-serve, from launch |
| Individual and lifelong learners | The learner | Accessible study help, course organization, assessment preparation | Free, later optional Premium | Self-serve, from launch |

The first ideal customer is a private higher education department or training center with 100–1,000 learners, a motivated academic decision-maker, and existing digital course material. This size range is a hypothesis to test in the first sales conversations.

## Value propositions

EdNova's core promise is course-grounded learning support in the student's language, with the educator kept in control. These are intended benefits until pilots measure them.

| Audience | What they get | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Students | AI companion across French, English, Arabic, Tunisian Darija and Arabizi, with sources and professor material first | Help matches the course and the way students actually ask questions |
| Students | Three progressive hints, symbolic math checking and mastery updates | Students work toward the answer before seeing a full solution |
| Students | Knowledge Constellation showing concepts, prerequisites and mastery | Progress and gaps are visible at a glance |
| Students | Assessment preparation based on deadlines, available time and weak topics | Revision time goes to what matters most |
| Students | Real-time whiteboard and group voice chat, with AI assistance on paid plans | Peer learning stays connected to the course |
| Educators | Material uploads, AI answer review, concept-tag correction and class progress | Educators control what the AI uses and can correct it |
| Institutions | Concept-level insight, multilingual support and a direct connection to their existing LMS | A learning layer that adds to existing systems instead of replacing them |

### Competitive position

The main alternatives are free general AI tools and the LMS an institution already runs. EdNova's case rests on combining these capabilities in one course-aware workflow; no single feature is the moat.

| Alternative | What it does well | Where EdNova must prove more value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| General AI assistants (such as ChatGPT or Gemini) | Free or low-cost explanations on almost any topic | Professor-first answers, educator review, mastery tracking |
| Source-grounded notebooks (such as NotebookLM) | Answers based on uploaded documents | Shared class material, practice, progress data and Darija support |
| LMS platforms (such as Moodle) | Course administration, enrollment and grading records | Personalized practice and concept-level insight, working alongside the LMS |
| Collaboration tools (such as Discord or Miro) | Communication and shared boards | Collaboration connected to course content and learning progress |

This comparison is a positioning view, not a claim that competitors lack these capabilities. It needs a feature-level review before external use.

Positioning line: **Beyond managing courses. Connecting the learning experience.**

## LMS integration

EdNova is an added learning layer: the institution keeps its LMS, and students open EdNova from their existing courses. The LMS remains the system of record for enrollment, official course administration and final grades, while EdNova handles AI support, practice, collaboration and concept-level insight.

| Capability | What it does for the institution | Technical route |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Launch from the course | Students and educators open EdNova from an LMS course without a second login | LTI 1.3 |
| Roster and role sync | Course members and their roles stay aligned with the LMS | LTI Names and Role Provisioning Services |
| Course placement | Educators add EdNova activities and resources to a course | LTI Deep Linking |
| Grade return | Educators can choose to send selected EdNova scores to the LMS gradebook | LTI Assignment and Grade Services |
| Course material | Educators bring existing files into EdNova with file import first, then deeper sync where needed | File import, then LMS-specific APIs |
| Institution-wide login | Staff and students use their institutional identity outside LMS launches | SAML or OpenID Connect |

LTI 1.3 comes first because one standard can serve several LMS platforms. The first connector should target the LMS used by the first pilot customers, and Moodle is the first candidate to check in buyer interviews. Each platform still needs its own testing before it is promised to a customer.

## Channels and customer relationships

Institutions are reached through direct, relationship-led sales, while educators and learners arrive through self-serve product use. The free tier also creates a bottom-up route: active student and educator use gives the sales team evidence to bring to an institution.

| Segment | How we reach them | How we onboard them | What keeps them |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Universities | Faculty introductions, academic events, live demos on real course material, paid pilots | Guided setup, training, account management | Measured learning value and periodic account reviews |
| Training centers | Direct outreach, partner referrals, cohort demos | Lightweight setup and responsive support | Cohort outcomes and day-to-day usefulness |
| High schools | School-group outreach and education partners | Structured rollout and teacher support | Curriculum fit, teacher adoption, student engagement |
| Independent educators | Website, educator referrals, communities | Self-service with optional help | Easy class management |
| Individual learners | Website, campus ambassadors, student clubs, shared learning spaces | In-product guidance | Personalization, saved progress, study routines |

The institutional sales path runs from outreach to discovery, a demo with real course material, a paid pilot with agreed success measures, an annual contract, and then renewal and expansion.

Account reviews cover active learners, educator usage, AI reliability, support requests and the agreed success measures. The professor review queue also serves as a relationship feature, because institutions get a clear process for reporting and correcting questionable answers.

## Revenue and packaging

Institutional licenses are the financial foundation, supported by educator subscriptions and one-time implementation fees. An optional individual premium plan can follow once willingness to pay is demonstrated.

### Plans

Free keeps core learning accessible, while AI-assisted collaboration and full assessment preparation are reserved for paid plans.

| Capability | Free | Educator | Campus | Enterprise |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Audience | Individuals | Tutors and small cohorts | Universities, schools, training centers | Larger organizations and networks |
| Pricing | Free | Monthly or annual subscription | Annual license | Custom contract |
| Collaborative whiteboard | Yes, no AI access | Yes, with AI | Yes, with AI | Yes, with AI |
| Group voice chat | Yes, no AI | Yes, with AI | Yes, with AI | Yes, with AI |
| Assessment preparation | Limited access | Full | Full | Full |
| AI companion and practice | Capped usage | Included | Included | Included |
| Knowledge Constellation | Core | Full | Full | Full |
| Class management and shared resources | No | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Educator review and class progress | No | Group level | Full | Full |
| Institutional administration | No | No | Yes | Advanced |
| LMS integration and service commitments | No | No | Standard LTI connector included | Custom integrations and agreed service commitments |

Free-tier caps should be set from measured cost per active user. These plans are proposed commercial packages, not completed product tiers.

### Other revenue

- **Implementation services:** one-time fees for custom LMS work, data migration, custom configuration and staff training.
- **Optional individual premium:** paid access to the features limited on Free, introduced only if demand is demonstrated.

| Scenario | Institutions | Learners per institution | Recurring revenue (TND/year) | Onboarding (TND) | First-year total (TND) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

## Costs and unit economics

At 40 TND per learner per year, a 70% gross margin leaves 12 TND per licensed learner per year, or 1 TND per month, for direct service costs. Real AI and infrastructure costs must be measured against that ceiling before prices are finalized.

| Target gross margin | Direct cost ceiling (TND/learner/year) | Direct cost ceiling (TND/learner/month) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 60% | 16.00 | 1.33 |
| 70% | 12.00 | 1.00 |
| 80% | 8.00 | 0.67 |

The margin target and the 40 TND price are planning assumptions. Licensed learners who rarely log in lower the average cost, so the plan should track cost per licensed learner and per active learner separately.

### Cost structure

| Cost category | Behavior | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Engineering and product | Mainly fixed | Developers, maintenance, product design |
| AI inference | Variable | Model calls, OCR, embeddings |
| Cloud hosting | Mixed | Compute, storage, database, networking |
| Real-time collaboration | Usage-dependent | Whiteboard sync, voice sessions |
| Content processing | Variable | Document indexing, OCR, storage |
| Institutional onboarding | Per customer | Configuration, training, migration |
| Sales and marketing | Mixed | Outreach, demos, events |
| Security and compliance | Mixed | Audits, policies, legal support |
| Customer support | Mixed | Helpdesk, account management |
| LMS integration | Mixed | Connector development, platform testing, maintenance |

Free-tier costs count as customer acquisition spending. Removing AI from the free whiteboard and voice chat limits inference cost, but voice and real-time sessions still need infrastructure and should be budgeted.

Currency exposure also needs tracking: local contracts may be paid in TND while AI and cloud services may be billed in foreign currency.

### Metrics to track per account

```latex
\text{Gross margin} = \frac{\text{Revenue} - \text{Direct service costs}}{\text{Revenue}} \times 100
```

```latex
\text{CAC payback} = \frac{\text{Customer acquisition cost}}{\text{Monthly gross profit per customer}}
```

```latex
\text{AI cost per active learner} = \frac{\text{Total AI inference cost}}{\text{Monthly active learners}}
```

## Risks and mitigations

The largest commercial risk is that institutions like the product but do not see enough added value to pay and renew. Paid pilots with agreed measures are the main test of that risk.

| Risk | Potential impact | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Institutions see too little value beyond free AI tools or their LMS | Low willingness to pay | Pilot around a defined problem and measurable outcomes |
| AI and infrastructure costs exceed the per-learner ceiling | Negative or thin margins | Measure cost per learner, use routing and caching, adjust pricing and usage caps |
| Peak usage degrades service, such as before exams | Poor experience and lost trust | Load testing, capacity planning, autoscaling, multi-provider fallback |
| AI gives inaccurate academic explanations | Loss of educator trust | Source grounding, math verification, review workflows |
| Dependence on one AI provider | Price, policy or availability shocks | Keep the model layer portable across providers |
| Free tier grows faster than revenue | Rising cost without payment | Usage caps, cost monitoring, clear upgrade paths |
| Free use replaces institutional purchases | Lower license demand | Keep educator oversight, class insight and administration in paid plans |
| Student engagement fades after the novelty | Low renewal potential | Build recurring study routines and measure retention |
| Educators find the platform burdensome | Low classroom adoption | Simplify course imports and provide onboarding |
| Procurement takes longer than expected | Cash-flow pressure | Smaller paid pilots and training-center customers |
| Learner data is mishandled, especially for minors | Legal and reputational harm | Role-based access, data minimization, security reviews, minor safeguards before school rollout |
| Foreign-currency costs rise against TND revenue | Margin pressure | Track exposure, build it into pricing, review it at renewal |
| Competitors copy visible features | Reduced differentiation | Focus on integrated workflows, institutional relationships and demonstrated results |
| LMS versions or institutional setups behave differently | Delayed rollouts and more support work | Build on LTI 1.3, test on each customer's setup before launch, keep file import as a fallback |

## Commercial readiness and validation

The prototype covers the core learning experience, but institutional sales require verified accounts, real roster data, broader curriculum and production infrastructure. Status below reflects the product reference and has not been independently audited.

| Area | Status | Required before institutional sale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Core learning experience | Described as implemented | Validate with real course material and learners |
| Collaborative learning | Described as implemented | Test whiteboard and voice at classroom scale |
| Educator tools | Partially validated | Confirm dashboards, material uploads and answer review in real use |
| Authentication and permissions | Needs verification | Secure real accounts and enforce student and educator roles |
| Institutional data | Incomplete | Live rosters, groups, enrollment and class assignment workflows |
| Curriculum coverage | Limited to Calculus I derivatives in the reference | Expand to the courses sold in the first pilots |
| Production capacity | To be built | Production environment, load testing, usage quotas, monitoring, support commitments |
| Plan enforcement | To be built | Feature access and usage caps for each plan |
| LMS interoperability | Planned | LTI 1.3 launch, roster sync and file import tested on the first customer's LMS |
