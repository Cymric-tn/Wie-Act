## Sign of the derivative and monotonicity
<!-- concept: variations -->
The sign of the derivative tells you whether a function goes up or down. On an interval where f'(x) > 0, the function f is increasing: when x grows, f(x) grows. Where f'(x) < 0, f is decreasing. Where f'(x) = 0 on a whole interval, f is constant.

To study the variations of f, compute f'(x), find where it equals zero, and build a sign table of f'(x). Then draw the variation table: an upward arrow where the derivative is positive and a downward arrow where it is negative.

## Finding a maximum or a minimum
<!-- concept: extremum -->
A local maximum or minimum can only happen where the derivative is zero or does not exist. If f'(x) changes from positive to negative at x = a, then f has a local maximum at a: it goes up, then down. If f'(x) changes from negative to positive, f has a local minimum.

A zero of the derivative without a change of sign is not an extremum: for f(x) = x³, f'(0) = 0 but f is increasing everywhere.

## Worked example
<!-- concept: extremum -->
Study f(x) = x² − 4x + 1. Its derivative is f'(x) = 2x − 4. It equals zero when x = 2, it is negative for x < 2 and positive for x > 2.

So f is decreasing on (−∞, 2] and increasing on [2, +∞). The function has a minimum at x = 2, and the minimum value is f(2) = 4 − 8 + 1 = −3.
